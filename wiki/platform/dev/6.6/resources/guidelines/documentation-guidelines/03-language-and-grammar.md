## What it is
Detailed language and grammar rules for Shopware documentation, covering voice, tense, abbreviations, capitalization, spelling, and punctuation.

## Key steps / config
- Use American English (refer to the [Cambridge dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-american-english/) for vocabulary and spelling).
- Voice principles: **Friendly** (less formal, occasionally funny), **Direct and clear** (to the point, skimmable), **Customer focussed** (assume knowledgeable but varying proficiency).
- Prefer active voice over passive, e.g. "The user passes the access-key" instead of "The access-key is passed by the user". Passive voice is acceptable to emphasize an object over an action, de-emphasize a subject, or when the doer is unknown.
- Prefer second person (*you*) over first person (*we*/*I*), except first-person is allowed for FAQs; use imperative with implicit *you* for instructions, e.g. "Create a PDF file" not "You need to create a PDF file". Avoid *our*.
- Use gender-neutral pronouns (*they*) and words (*humankind* instead of *mankind*).
- Spell out an unfamiliar abbreviation on first use with the abbreviation in parentheses, e.g. *JSON Web Token (JWT)*, then use the abbreviation only; common ones (*API, HTTPS, PDF, XML, PNG, HTML*) rarely need spelling out. Do not create abbreviations for product/feature names; always spell them out. Plural abbreviations add "s" (*APIs*) or "es" if ending in s/sh/ch/x (*OSes*). Do not invent custom abbreviations.
- Limit informal contractions (*it's, you're*) but use negation contractions (*isn't, don't, can't*) since they are easier to read correctly than "not".
- Use simple present tense; avoid future or past tense.
- Capitalize the first letter after a colon; follow standard capitalization for company/product/feature names.
- Base spellings on the [Cambridge dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/essential-american-english/); prefer spellings not affected by regional dictionary differences (e.g. *color* over *colour*).
- Don't use `/` as a conjunction (use *or*) or `&` (use *and*).
- Comma: use before the final conjunction in a series of three or more items; after an introductory phrase; before a conjunctive adverb (*otherwise, however*) with a comma after it; after the first of two independent clauses joined by a conjunction, unless both are very short.
- Use an em dash for a break in sentence flow; use a hyphen for prefixes, number ranges (*25-30 GB*), compound nouns, ambiguity removal (*logged-in*), or vowel-ending prefixes (*co-op*).
- End every sentence, including list items (except phrases), with a period; don't end headings or URLs with a period; place the period inside quotation marks when a sentence ends in quotes.
- Avoid slash-based date formats, slashes for fractions, and slashes to separate alternatives (*blue/red*).
- Don't hide important information in parentheses.

## Gotchas
- Don't start consecutive sentences with the same phrase such as *In order to, To do, You can*.
- Avoid buzzwords, jargon, idioms, and informal internet slang; avoid requesting words like *please* or *request*.
- Don't write the way you speak — add a pinch of formal style while conveying only the information the audience needs.
